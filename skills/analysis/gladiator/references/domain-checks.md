# Domain checks

Short checklists to support the five-axis assessment. Load only the sections that match the plan. Each item is a question to answer from evidence, not a rule to enforce. Items marked (convention) are common practice with no study behind them here.

## Research and information

- Is each load-bearing fact dated and sourced? Which ones rest on memory?
- Has anything about the present (prices, versions, roles, rules) been looked up in this session?
- Where two sources disagree, is the disagreement shown or averaged away?
- What is the one missing fact that would most change the plan?
- Is confidence stated per finding, with what would change it?

## Planning

- Is the end state specific and observable? Could a stranger tell whether it was reached?
- Are there phases with a decision point between them?
- Is there an if-then branch for the most likely thing to go wrong?
- Are the resources (time, money, people) counted against what the plan needs?
- What is the trigger to start, stated as a condition and not a feeling?
- Has the failure case been written out before committing (a pre-mortem)?

## Building software

- Is the core logic built and running before the layers around it? (convention)
- Is the smallest version that tests the main assumption defined?
- Is the simplest architecture that works being used, with a named reason for anything more? (convention)
- Are secrets kept out of code and logs?
- Is there a way to tell from logs what happened when it breaks?
- Is there a check that must pass before the work is called done?

## AI pipelines and prompts

- Does each stage have one job and a defined input and output?
- Is the hand-off between stages structured, so the next stage does not have to guess?
- What happens when a stage fails or returns nothing?
- Is there a human checkpoint before any action that cannot be undone?
- Has the output been tested against examples, including cases that should fail?
- Is the model for each stage chosen for the job, and is that choice current?

## Negotiation preparation

- Is the best alternative to an agreement decided before the conversation?
- Are the interests on both sides written down, not just the positions?
- Is there a package of items to trade, not a single number?
- Is the walk-away point fixed in advance?
- What does the other side need to be able to say yes?

## Things deliberately not included

The earlier version of this skill summarised other skills' material on market analysis briefs, on eliciting information from people and on managing what competitors are led to believe. Those sections were not carried over. A market brief is a single-domain product with its own skill. The interpersonal material described ways of steering people without their knowledge, which this skill does not recommend.
