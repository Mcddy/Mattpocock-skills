## 1. Gather the material

State the brief in a few lines: the audience, the decision or action they came for, the result they need, the platform, constraints, the accepted brand, and what is real versus sample.

Then list the particulars. Design can't add specificity that isn't here.

- **Supplied:** content, names, numbers, dates, prices, constraints and brand assets.
- **Observed:** look at each asset, record or product at the size users will see it, at three distances: thumbnail (what's recognisable), arm's length (structure) and close up (detail and material). Note what's distinctive, awkward or surprising.
- **Domain truths:** facts a reader could check.
- **Friction:** what makes the decision hard, what an expert checks first, what newcomers get wrong, and any inconvenient fact to face rather than smooth over.
- **Gaps:** what an expert would expect that you don't have.

If the user is present and a gap would change credibility, ask once for the few that matter. Otherwise proceed with what's true. Where a missing fact bears on a decision, show its absence at that decision ("Delivery times not yet set") rather than padding around it.

Thin material sets a ceiling. Choose an idea that works with fewer, larger, exact things.



## 2. Find the organising idea

For substantial work with an open direction, write three candidate ideas, each from a different source. Skip this for small edits:

- **The object:** the thing itself becomes the structure.
- **The decision:** the hardest judgement is made visible and operable.
- **The context:** the moment, place, device or pace of use shapes the work.

For each idea, give one sentence, what a viewer sees first, what changes in layout, copy and behaviour, and what it gives up. A palette, mood or style isn't an idea, and variants with the same structure count as one idea.

Choose the idea that:

- eases the hard decision;
- uses the strongest particulars;
- fails the noun swap (rename the product and the design no longer fits);
- is honest about the material;
- can be built at quality within scope.

Choose the architecture for the question: a sequence for a process, a comparison for a choice, a grid for browsing like items. None is the default.

Write the idea in one sentence, plus the line a stranger should say after a five-second look. If the user has accepted a direction, work inside it, and reopen it only on request or rejection.
