## 8. Verify

Choose checks in proportion to the work.

- **Interactive:** if you can render, inspect desktop and phone, including 320 CSS pixel reflow. Exercise the primary journey, failure and recovery; keyboard order and activation; dismissal and focus return; touch targets; long content; contrast; text enlargement; and reduced motion.
- **Static work and exports:** inspect the intended surface, ratio, platform crop and thumbnail. For portfolio captures, judge the interface and the wrapper separately; the wrapper mustn't crowd or crop the work.
- **Layout:** at each target width, short headings and labels shouldn't strand one word while space remains. Fix the cap, never cut a needed qualifier, and allow wraps where meaning requires them. Each lead-in shares a group with its target, and no input reads as data.
- **Performance:** measure only when it is load-bearing, in a named environment. Never invent metrics.

Use existing tooling and authorised surfaces, and honour tool errors that forbid workarounds. If you can't render, finish the source and name the visual checks that remain. Selected checks aren't certification.



## 9. Judge and revise

Functional checks don't establish quality. After they pass, apply the lenses below. They are agent review, not independent judgement.

1. **Cold read:** at thumbnail and full size, before rereading the brief, write one line saying what it is. If that misses the five-second line or fits an unrelated product, fix the framing that makes it interchangeable: the headline, lead image or composition. Familiar structures that do a job stay; specificity goes into what they carry.
2. **Median:** describe the typical solution in three lines and list where this work departs from it. Each departure needs a reason from the subject. Departures only in palette, typeface or decoration are the median in costume.
3. **Reason:** for the five most prominent decisions, a reason that would fit any product marks a default.
4. **Expert and buyer:** ask what looks naive, missing or wrong to the domain expert and to the actual user. Fix what's true. Turn missing facts into reported gaps, never inventions.
5. **Strip:** remove effects. If hierarchy collapses, rebuild the structure. Then remove what nobody would miss.
6. **Fact map:** for each state, list every fact a user acts on and where it appears. Each gets one primary home at decision size beside its action, with history as support. A second appearance needs an independent context; a headline answer restated by a timeline at another scale is duplication.
7. **Copy:** check transplant (could a sentence move unchanged to an unrelated piece?), swap, source (every specific traces to the particulars), voice, and delta against any supplied original.
8. **Close range:** check character details, breaks, crops, alignment, focus treatment, and empty and edge states.

Each criticism names an observed problem, its consequence and a checkable fix. Verify it before acting, and don't invent defects.

When understanding or task success is the claim, measure correct interpretation, consequential errors, recovery, time and transfer separately. Speed, low effort, confidence or preference don't certify understanding, and published effects don't predict this product's results. Consult `evidence.md` when a choice rests on research.

Then revise:

- Fix the highest-impact problem first.
- If a substantial open-direction brief remains correct but interchangeable after repairs, revisit the organising idea. Preserve accepted directions and useful functional conventions; novelty alone does not justify redesign.
- When named defects are fixed and the owner still finds the work below the bar, build a candidate from a different idea with the same content, and compare the two at the same sizes.
- In a defect-only or export check of accepted work, fix the defects and leave the direction alone.



## Common failure modes

- Polish over thin material, or invented particulars to fill it.
- A style chosen first, with content poured in.
- An idea stated in the headline but absent from layout and behaviour.
- One decision fact shown at several scales, or the operative fact demoted to a footnote.
- Decoration carrying hierarchy, and motion explaining nothing.
- Fake roughness or a "human touch" standing in for real particulars.
- Stock voice overcorrected into curt or combative copy.
- Limits hidden in a footer, or repeated beside every control.



## Adversarial test (illustrative)

**Brief:** a preorder page for a small bakery. Supplied: nine items with prices, pickup on Friday from 7 to 11 am, three photos, the owner's own description of the sourdough, and no reviews. The owner asks for "social proof and a human touch". Ordering isn't connected yet.

- **Fails if it** invents testimonials or a founding story, adds hand-drawn wobble as false proof of human origin or an "AI-free" badge, repeats the pickup window in a decorative timeline, or lets the order button look live.
- **Passes if it** shows the photos at a size where crust and crumb read, carries the owner's sourdough wording, and places the pickup window beside the order action with a plain note there that orders aren't open yet. It reports the missing reviews as a gap, with a route to collecting real ones.
