# A daily fermented oat drink and winter colds in working adults: a randomised trial

The FOD-7 Trial Writing Group (five authors; affiliations in the funding statement)

Published 16 March 2026

## Abstract

**Background.** Colds cost working days every winter. FOD-7 is a fermented oat drink taken once a day.

**Methods.** We randomised 2,400 adults to FOD-7 daily for 12 weeks or to their usual diet. The primary outcome was a week with three or more days of cold symptoms.

**Results.** 68 percent of the FOD-7 group and 90 percent of the usual diet group completed 12 weeks. FOD-7 reduced colds by 40 percent (p = 0.03). The drink was well tolerated.

**Conclusion.** A daily fermented oat drink reduces colds in adults and can be recommended as a simple preventive measure.

## Methods

### Participants

Between 8 September and 17 October 2025 we enrolled 2,400 employees aged 18 to 45 at two distribution sites of one logistics company. People with a long-term illness, and anyone already taking a fermented food product, were excluded.

### Randomisation and allocation

Participants were allocated 1:1 by a computer-generated list. The usual diet group received no drink and no placebo, so participants and site staff knew the allocation.

### Outcomes

The trial was registered before recruitment (registry entry SYN-2025-0417). The registered primary outcome was a respiratory infection confirmed by a general practitioner within 12 weeks. Because confirmed infections were fewer than expected, the statistical analysis plan was amended in February 2026, after follow-up had ended, to make symptom days the primary outcome: at least one week with three or more days of cold symptoms, recorded by the participant in a weekly online diary. Days off work were a secondary outcome.

### Analysis

Participants who stopped taking the drink were withdrawn and not followed further. The analysis included everyone who completed 12 weeks. Proportions were compared with a chi-squared test.

## Discussion

FOD-7 lowered the proportion of adults with a symptomatic cold by 40 percent over a winter. The effect on infections confirmed by a general practitioner was in the same direction. Adults who want fewer colds can add the drink to their diet.

The trial has limitations. It ran for one winter in one company. More participants left the FOD-7 group than the usual diet group.
