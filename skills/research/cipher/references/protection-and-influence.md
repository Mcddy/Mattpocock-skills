# Protection and influence

## Protecting information

Tiers:
1. Protect absolutely: future positions, unreleased products, strategic plans, financial parameters, analytical frameworks.
2. Protect significantly: competitive findings, methodology, business-development targets, capability gaps.
3. Manage carefully: general direction, current priorities, key relationships.
4. Managed public: published work, demonstrated capabilities, general philosophy.

For each critical item: how could someone learn it (human sources, digital traces, inference, third-party disclosure), what denial measure applies, what residual vulnerability remains. Every person with access is another route out; apply need-to-know. The commonest leak is casual conversation, not hostile collection.

Compartments: full access (two or three people: plans, resources, vulnerabilities, real positions); professional access (capabilities, general direction, track record); public (core identity and published work). Information shared at one level should be fit for the level above it.

OPSEC loop: identify critical information; identify who would benefit from it; identify how they could collect it; assess risk (probability times impact); apply specific countermeasures.

Concealment is fine: not disclosing, need-to-know, timing of announcements, silence. Do not create false impressions (see the rules in SKILL.md).

## Influence campaign (true information only)

1. Identify: the audience, their current beliefs and values, their trusted sources, and what must be true for them to accept the message.
2. Objectives: the specific belief, decision or action, a measurable indicator, a timeline.
3. Campaign: narrative, proof points, channels, messengers, sequence (what must be believed before the next belief is possible).
4. Measure: signs of belief change and signs of resistance.

Narrative: one paragraph which, if believed, produces the outcome; three factual proof points; what could undermine it and how to pre-empt; who carries it and why the audience trusts them.

Perception gap: current perception (honestly), desired perception (specific and achievable), the gap, and a sequence of exposures and proof points over time.

## Positioning

- One-sentence identity statement that places Shaun correctly.
- Supporting elements: track record, demonstrated (not merely certified) skill, social proof, origin story.
- Verification check: what does someone find when they research him, and does it match?
- Audience emphasis of the same authentic person: financial and trading (Cerberus, analytical rigour); AI and technology (prompt engineering, agent architecture, commercial AI work); clients (results and delivery).
