# Honest elicitation and deception check

## Limits for elicitation

Only on topics the person is free to discuss and that are fair for you to ask about (their views, experience, advice, public work). Do not seek confidential employer or client information, or private personal details. Do not misstate facts, claim a false identity or invent a reason for the conversation. If they deflect or decline, stop.

## Techniques

1. **Acknowledge expertise.** "You know this space better than most. What is the real picture?" Recognition plus an open question invites explanation.
2. **Tentative view.** State a view you hold loosely and say so: "My read is X, but I may be missing something." People correct and expand. Never state something you know to be false.
3. **Plain question.** Ask the basic question you would like explained. People share readily with someone learning. Do not pretend to know less than you do.
4. **Mutual sharing.** Give something genuinely useful first, then ask for their read. Reciprocity does the work. Share only what is true and yours to share.
5. **Check what you heard.** "I heard a version of X. Is that the full picture?" They confirm or correct.
6. **Considered challenge.** Offer a view they may want to push back on, politely, and listen to the correction.
7. **Question in flow.** Do not lead with the key question. Build rapport, ask it mid-conversation, then carry on past it.

## Plan

```
WHAT I WANT TO UNDERSTAND:
TECHNIQUE: which fits this person and setting
REASON FOR THE CONVERSATION: the real one
OPENING / TRANSITION / THE QUESTION / CLOSE
IF IT DOES NOT WORK: alternative route (a different person, a public source)
```

## Deception check

Look for cognitive load and consistency failures, not "tells".

DIRTS:
- Discrepancy: internal inconsistencies, contradicts known facts, words versus actions.
- Incongruence: emotion does not fit the content, detail level mismatch.
- Reaction: unprompted defensiveness, qualifications piling up, changing the subject.
- Timing: unusual delays on particular questions, over-prepared answers, changes of pace.
- Scope: unexplained gaps, avoiding a time period, resisting elaboration.

Verbal indicators worth noting: distancing language ("that person" for a name), qualified denials ("I don't think I would ever" for "I didn't"), over-emphasis, answering with a question, passive construction on the key content, invoking reputation in place of evidence, rich detail everywhere except the key claim.

Baseline method:
1. Establish normal behaviour in a non-sensitive conversation (pace, eye contact, gestures, detail, emotional range).
2. Introduce the topic and watch for change.
3. Ask calibration questions to which you already know the answer.
4. Ask the target questions and compare with both baseline and calibration.

Deviations are indicators, not proof. Stress is not deception. Triangulate with documents or independent sources, and do not accuse on indicators alone.
