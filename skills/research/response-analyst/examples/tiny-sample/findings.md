# Roster pulse check: findings from six respondents

Response analysis: counts over named denominators, with intervals.

SYNTHETIC data. Written by the authoring session from `analysis.txt` in this folder. Not independently checked.

## Answer

Short notice is the one thing these six agree on. Post the roster a week ahead and stop changing it afterwards: it is cheap, it can be undone, and it is what they asked for. Everything else here is too thin to rank.

- FACT. 4 of the 5 who answered named short notice as what makes a shift hard to attend; 1 of 6 skipped the question. The 95% interval runs from 38% to 96%: with five answers the real share could be a minority or nearly everyone.
- FACT. 4 of 6 would swap to a fixed weekly shift, 2 would not, nobody was unsure. Interval 30% to 90%.
- FACT. 4 of the 5 who answered agreed or strongly agreed that the new roster works for them (Strongly agree 1, Agree 3, Disagree 1, Strongly disagree 0); 1 of 6 skipped.

## Who answered

6 of the 9 people on the roster, asked in person by the coordinator at the end of a shift during one week. GAP: the 3 who were not on shift were not asked. Answers were given face to face to the person who writes the roster, which may have softened them.

## Newer against longer-serving volunteers

Both newer volunteers would swap to a fixed shift (2 of 2), against 2 of the 4 longer-serving ones. That reads as 100% against 50%, and it is two people against two people. The difference is 50 points with a 95% interval from minus 24 to plus 85. These six answers do not show which group is keener, and one more answer either way would change the picture.

## What they wrote

All four comments, in full. R03 and R06 wrote none.

- R01: "Send the roster a week earlier."
- R02: "Stop changing it after it is posted."
- R04: "Nothing, it is fine."
- R05: "A week of notice would fix most of it."

With four comments, coding them into themes would add nothing, so they are all here. None names a person or a role. R04 is the case that does not fit the rest: one of the six has no complaint.

## What six answers can and cannot carry

- They can support a cheap, reversible change that most of them asked for, in the tick-boxes (4 of 5) and in their own words (R01, R02, R05).
- They cannot say what "the volunteers" think, compare newer with longer-serving people, or stand as a percentage without its count beside it.
- GAP. Whether the three who were not asked have the same difficulty. Asking them takes three conversations.
