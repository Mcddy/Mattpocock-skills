## Who answered

All 31 people present sat the paper quiz (31 of 31). People enrolled but absent that day did not. 22 of 31 answered every item. There were 9 omitted answers across six items (k1 2, k2 1, k5 2, k8 1, k10 1, k12 2); an omitted item is scored as not correct and counted separately.

## How the figures were made

Score: one point for each keyed answer, out of 12. Minimum 2, median 7, maximum 11.

Upper group: the 8 respondents scoring 9 or more. Lower group: the 8 scoring 5 or fewer. (27 per cent of 31 is 8; tied scores are kept together.) D is the share correct in the upper group minus the share correct in the lower.

With 8 in each group, one respondent changing one answer moves D by 0.12. Differences of 0.1 or 0.2 between items mean nothing here. These figures describe how the items behaved for this group on this day.

## The candidates, read against the questions

**k7, possible miskey.** FACT: 19 of 31 chose B, including 6 of the 8 top scorers; 6 of 31 chose the keyed C, including 2 of the 8. The script raises three candidates on it: nearly everyone wrong, a wrong option preferred by the upper group, and possible miskey. Reading the item settles it: MEDIAN is the middle value, so B is correct and the key is wrong. This is a fault in the key, not in the question or the respondents.

After correcting the key (`analysis-after-key-fix.txt`): k7 is correct for 19 of 31 (61%), 10 of 11 in the upper group and 3 of 11 in the lower, D = +0.64. Nineteen people gain a point and the six who chose C lose one. The median score moves from 7 to 8, and the groups change to 11 scoring 9 or more and 11 scoring 6 or fewer, so every other item's D shifts a little. Any grade boundary set on the old scores needs redoing.

**k10, dead option.** FACT: D was chosen by 0 of 31; the wrong answers went to B (5) and C (2). The item still separates the groups (8 of 8 against 3 of 8), so it is usable as it stands. A fourth option that somebody might believe would make it a four-way choice instead of a three-way one.

**k1, nearly everyone right.** FACT: 29 of 31, with 2 omitted and no wrong option chosen at all. All three of its "option nobody chose" candidates come from that. Nothing is wrong with it as a first question. It should not be counted on to distinguish anyone.

**k8, not flagged, worth a read.** ASSESSMENT, low confidence. 5 of 8 top scorers got it right against 3 of 8 in the lower group, and its correlation with the rest of the quiz is +0.04. The wording ("sorted on its own") may be read two ways. The difference from a typical item here is two people, so this is a prompt to reread the question and nothing more.

**k11, after the key fix.** With the regrouped scores the script raises a candidate on option B: 4 of 11 in the upper group chose it against 2 of 11 in the lower, the same number of top scorers as chose the keyed C. The key is right (COUNT counts numbers). ASSESSMENT, low confidence: a hard item on which stronger respondents were guessing between two options. No action on the item; it marks something to teach.

## What these responses cannot show

- GAP. How these items would behave with another group. Thirty-one people give wide intervals: k6's 16 of 31 correct is 52%, 95% interval 35% to 68%.
- GAP. Why anyone chose a wrong option. That takes asking a few of them.
- The scores are counts of keyed answers on a twelve-item quiz. They are not a measure of anyone's ability and do not label anyone.
