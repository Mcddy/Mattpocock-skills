# Deslop

Stock prose breaks down at a predictable point: a ready-made phrase stands where an observation should be. "The signal here is loud" replaces what was actually seen. "Seamless" replaces what no longer gets in the way. To fix it, find what is true and particular, decide what it means for this reader, and write that.

The goal is not shorter text, a harder tone or a cleaner word list. A technical term used in its technical sense (signal, noise, uncertainty, robust) stays. Swapping one stock voice for another is also a failure, whether that means clipped fragments, contrarian swagger or prose scrubbed flat. Phrase swaps come last, and on their own they fix nothing.

Scale the method to the job. A label or error message needs sections 3 and 4 and one careful read. An essay, case study or landing page needs all of it.

## 1. Gather the material

Name the reader and what they need to decide, do or understand next. For small edits, infer this and proceed. Ask only when a missing fact would change the result.

Collect what you can use:

- supplied facts: names, numbers, dates, constraints, results and quotes;
- what a supplied render, image or dataset visibly contains;
- settled domain knowledge a reader could check;
- the writer's own vocabulary and phrasing.

Write, privately, the one sentence the piece must leave with the reader. If you can't, the draft is short of substance, not style. Use what exists, flag the gap or ask one precise question. Never fill a gap with plausible anecdotes, clients, numbers, quotes, benefits or expertise. Vivid invented detail reads like evidence and isn't.

Protect the following unless asked to change them: quotations, code, commands, identifiers, URLs, citations, legal and safety notices, and exact source text. Preserve facts, uncertainty, attribution, scope and distinctions.

Order and framing are not protected. Restructure when a draft leads with its own process instead of the reader's task.

## 2. Find the point

For substantial pieces, decide four things:

- the question the piece answers;
- the observation that makes it worth reading;
- the evidence that earns its conclusion;
- what it leaves out.

When the direction is open, compare structures rather than rewordings: result first, problem first, one case, a comparison or a sequence. Pick the one that fits the reader's actual question. Keep a direction the writer has already chosen.

## 3. Write from the material

- **Lead with the thing:** the object, result, action or finding. Cut warm-ups, announcements of what the piece will do and recaps of what it just did.
- **Name the instance.** Write the named tool rather than "solutions", or the measured change rather than "improved efficiency", when those facts were supplied. A relevant example can make an abstraction usable. A hypothetical or fictional example is fine when the task permits it and it is clearly labelled; never present one as evidence, a real client or a measured result.
- **Show or cut evaluation.** A word that asserts a quality ("powerful", "seamless") needs the observable consequence that makes it true, or it goes. A phrase that announces significance ("here's the key insight") usually stands in for the insight, so state the insight instead. Keep words that are precise in context or quoted.
- **Hedge to the actual uncertainty,** once, where it applies. Keep real limits. Drop stacked, reflexive hedges.
- **Repeat with a purpose.** Repetition makes a claim feel truer without supporting it. Repeat for orientation, learning, consistent naming or a separate decision point.
- **Recommend with reasons.** Give the reason and the real trade-off. Leave out guilt, threats, false urgency and fake alternatives. Direct instructions are right in task copy. Don't cast the reader as resistant or write against an imagined sceptic.
- **Keep the writer's voice:** their vocabulary, sentence length and humour, including wit and imagery that carry meaning. Add no persona, slang or swagger.
- **Let content set the shape.** A triple, a "not X but Y", a rhetorical question or a one-line paragraph is fine when the content has that shape. It is stock when it's there only for rhythm.
- **Simplify without merging.** Plainer words help only when distinctions survive: "may" stays "may", "most" stays "most", and two different fees stay two fees. Success means the reader interprets and acts correctly, not that the text feels simpler.

Follow the user's spelling and punctuation conventions. When a mark is ruled out, rewrite the relationship with a full stop, comma, colon or new sentence rather than a lookalike character. Leave protected text alone.

## 4. Interface and portfolio copy

Read every visible string in order as one text, across the states the journey reaches: loading, empty, error, success and the result of the main action.

- **Give each string its job.** The headline names the thing or offer. Labels name. Actions are a verb and object that predict the result. Status reports state. Errors say what happened and how to recover. Copy that narrates the interface ("Use the options below to get started") usually signals a structure problem, so fix the structure first.
- **One home per decision fact.** A fact that changes the next action (an earlier departure, a deadline, a cost) goes in a main sentence at the decision, at body size or larger. History and provenance can be supporting detail; the operative fact can't. Repeat it only for an independent context such as a review dialog, receipt or required notice. A headline answer restated by a timeline at another scale is one fact shown twice.
- **Keep roles distinct.** Word user input, observations, computed results and suggestions so none can pass for another. A chosen 7:30 should read "Leaving after 7:30", not look like another service.
- **Group lead-ins with targets.** End a label with a colon only when what it introduces follows in the same group.
- **Let length follow meaning.** Don't cut a needed qualifier to fit a line. If a short label leaves one word alone while its container has room, the cause is usually an arbitrary width cap, so fix the cap. Let labels wrap where meaning or the platform requires it.
- **Keep names consistent.** Use the same name across a choice, its preview, summary, receipt and recovery.
- **Distinguish alternatives.** Describe each option by what makes someone choose it over its sibling. If two descriptions could swap and stay true, find the missing distinction in the material. Facts the options share stay shared.
- **Match promises to behaviour.** If the copy says compare, save or undo and the product doesn't, revise the promise and flag the missing capability rather than quietly shrinking the brief.
- **Place limits where they matter.** State each material limit once, where it changes a decision. Keep sample, fictional or prototype status clear wherever a reader could be misled. Never turn drafts into orders, fixtures into customers, practice work into client engagements, or review-pending records into import-ready ones.
- **No automatic AI disclosures or process badges.** Follow the user's disclosure choice, and never claim human authorship or experience that didn't happen.
- **Respect native conventions.** Locale formats are conventions, not faults; change one only for a real comprehension problem. Keep build and QA vocabulary out of product copy unless users need it, and keep domain terms and provenance that help.
- **Order portfolio cases by importance.** Lead with the problem and what works, then the decision that mattered. Technical receipts support.

## 5. Review

Set criteria from the reader and brief before rereading the draft. Then check:

- **Transplant:** could the sentence move unchanged into an unrelated piece? Rewrite it from the material or cut it.
- **Swap:** can sibling descriptions trade places and stay true?
- **Source:** does every specific trace to the material? Specificity without a source is fabrication.
- **Delta:** compared with the original, are commitments, uncertainty, distinctions and scope intact?
- **Voice:** would this writer say it to this reader?
- **Overcorrection:** did the edit strip legitimate terms, cut context the reader needed, delete connecting reasoning or impose a new house style?

For any criticism, name the largest weakness, where it is, what it costs the reader and how to fix it, and confirm the criticism is real. Don't invent defects, and don't read a negative reaction as bias against AI. These checks are agent review, not a reader test or human approval.

For layout-bound copy, check the render if you can. If not, list the wrap and fit checks that remain.

Where comprehension carries risk (instructions, money, health, safety) and readers can be tested, measure correct interpretation and action. Preference and perceived ease are separate results. Consult `references/evidence.md` only when a claim about what plain or concrete writing achieves matters to the work.

## Common failure modes

- Polishing sentences in a piece that has no point yet.
- Inventing specifics to sound concrete.
- Scrubbing suspect words while the structure stays generic.
- Simplifying away a distinction the reader needed.
- Writing one idea three times, as eyebrow, headline and subtitle.
- Fixing a wrap by deleting a qualifier.

## Example (illustrative)

Material: 20 recorded onboarding sessions. Fourteen stopped at bank linking, which asks for an account number before explaining why.

- **Before:** "Ooh, the signal is here: users are craving a smoother, more seamless onboarding experience."
- **After:** "Fourteen of 20 recorded sessions stopped at bank linking, the step that asks for an account number before saying why."
- **Leave alone:** "Below 2 m the signal is weak relative to sensor noise," in a methods section. It is a technical claim with a condition.
- **Adversarial:** asked to make it punchier, "Bank linking kills onboarding." fails the delta check because it drops both the count and the cause.

## Origin screening

Run this only when the user explicitly asks for a human-versus-AI screen.

- Treat it as a separate criterion. Apply the user's rule exactly and record each literal answer.
- Treat a judge's reasons as hypotheses. Fix a real weakness they point to, never a surface tell.
- Never add errors or roughness, invent experience, remove scope or disclosure, coach a judge or claim an unobserved result.
- A screen never replaces this review.

## Output

Return the revised copy. Mention only unresolved facts, changes in meaning and any rationale the user asked for. If layout matters, say what was rendered and what remains untested.

The skill adds no access, permissions or approval rituals. Existing authority covers routine edits, and real tool approvals are honoured. Keep the original recoverable unless an in-place edit was requested. Nothing is published.
